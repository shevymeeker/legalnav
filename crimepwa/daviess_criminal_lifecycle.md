# Criminal Case Lifecycle in Daviess County, Kentucky
## Comprehensive Procedural Analysis

### Document Authority and Scope

This analysis governs criminal proceedings in the Daviess Circuit Court, 6th Judicial Circuit, pursuant to C6 Local Rules (approved March 22, 2012), Kentucky Rules of Criminal Procedure (RCr), Kentucky Revised Statutes (KRS), and applicable constitutional provisions. Daviess County operates under a two-division system (Division I and Division II), each handling both civil and criminal matters with dedicated Master and Deputy Master Commissioners.

---

## PHASE 1: PRE-ARREST / INVESTIGATION

### Law Enforcement Investigation
Crimes come to law enforcement attention through victim reports, witness statements, officer observation, or ongoing investigations. No formal court involvement exists during pure investigation phase. Officers may conduct interviews, collect physical evidence, execute search warrants, and build probable cause for arrest or complaint.

**Constitutional Framework:**
- Fourth Amendment (U.S. Constitution): Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures
- Section 10 (Kentucky Constitution): Warrant requirements, probable cause supported by oath or affirmation
- Search warrants must particularly describe persons, places, and items to be seized

**Investigatory Tools:**
- Interviews and interrogations
- Search warrants (requiring judicial approval based on probable cause affidavit)
- Consent searches
- Subpoenas for records
- Surveillance (subject to constitutional limitations)
- Confidential informants (KRE 508 governs identity privilege)

## PHASE 2: ARREST AND INITIAL CUSTODY

### Arrest Mechanisms

**Arrest with Warrant (RCr 2.04-2.06)**

Complaint Process:
- Officer or complainant files written complaint stating essential facts constituting the offense (RCr 2.02)
- Complaint must be supported by oath or affirmation
- Complaint presented to judge, trial commissioner, or county attorney for probable cause determination
- If probable cause found, judge issues arrest warrant

Warrant Requirements (Section 10, Kentucky Constitution; RCr 2.04):
- Must particularly describe person to be arrested "as nearly as may be"
- Must be based on probable cause
- Must be supported by oath or affirmation
- Officer need not have warrant in possession during arrest but must have copy

Service of Warrant:
- Copy of warrant and underlying complaint must be served on accused at time of arrest or as soon thereafter as practicable (RCr 2.06)
- If arrest made without physical warrant, arresting officer must obtain warrant and complaint "as soon thereafter as practicable" and serve defendant

**Arrest without Warrant (RCr 2.10; KRS 431.005)**

Felony Arrests - Officer may arrest without warrant when:
- Felony committed in officer's presence
- Officer has probable cause to believe person committed felony (even outside presence)
- Officer has reasonable grounds to believe felony has been committed and probable cause to believe person committed it

Misdemeanor Arrests - Officer may arrest without warrant when:
- Misdemeanor committed in officer's presence
- Specific statutory authorization exists (domestic violence, protective order violations, etc.)

Citation in Lieu of Arrest (KRS 431.005):
- Officer may issue citation for misdemeanors if reasonable grounds exist that person will appear
- If defendant fails to appear, complaint filed and warrant issued
- Physical arrest plus citation: officer marks "ARRESTED" on citation instead of court date

Post-Arrest Complaint Requirement (RCr 3.02):
- When arrest made without warrant, post-arrest complaint must be filed
- Complaint provides charging instrument for warrantless arrests
- Must state essential facts constituting offense
- Informs judge of reason accused is before court

**Bench Warrants**

Issued when witness fails to appear pursuant to subpoena or defendant fails to appear pursuant to summons. May be issued "from the bench" during proceedings. Governed by existing practice codified in RCr.

### Right to Contact Attorney

**RCr 2.14(1) - Immediate Rights**

Any person taken into custody must be allowed to communicate with attorney "as soon as practicable." This is not simply a courtesy but a mandatory procedural requirement. Denial of this right creates grounds for suppression of statements and potentially other remedies.

Practical implementation: Upon booking at Daviess County Detention Center, defendant must be afforded reasonable opportunity to contact counsel, family members who can contact counsel, or public defender's office.

### Booking and Pretrial Services

**Detention Center Processing:**
- Fingerprinting and photographing
- Criminal history check (local, statewide, national)
- Medical screening
- Property inventory
- Assignment to housing unit

**Pretrial Services Interview (within 12 hours of arrest):**
- Pretrial services officer conducts risk assessment interview
- Officer gathers extensive information: nature of charge, criminal history, employment, residence, family ties, substance abuse history, mental health status
- Risk assessment evaluates: likelihood of appearance at court proceedings, danger to public safety, likelihood of new criminal activity
- Officer prepares written recommendation for bond/release conditions
- Recommendation presented to judge at initial appearance/arraignment

## PHASE 3: INITIAL APPEARANCE BEFORE MAGISTRATE

### Appearance Without Unnecessary Delay

**RCr 3.02 - Timing Requirements**

Person arrested shall be taken before judge "without unnecessary delay." This constitutional requirement (Section 11, Kentucky Constitution; Fourth Amendment) ensures prompt judicial determination of detention legality.

If no judge available:
- All arrest documents given to jailer
- Jailer must give documents to clerk on or before next business day (RCr 3.02(3)(4))
- Defendant brought before judge at earliest opportunity

Video Arraignment: Permissible under RCr 8.02 and 8.28 if it is functional equivalent of in-person appearance (Commonwealth v. Ingram, 46 S.W.3d 569 (Ky. 2001))

### Rights Advisement at Initial Appearance

Judge must inform defendant of following rights (Kentucky Constitution Sections 7, 11, 16, 17; U.S. Constitution Sixth Amendment):

**Right to Counsel:**
- Right to be represented by attorney at all critical stages
- If defendant cannot afford attorney, court must appoint one for any charge that could result in confinement (KRS 31.110(1)(a); Argersinger v. Hamlin, 407 U.S. 25 (1972))
- Appointment determination made at arraignment based on indigency showing
- Partial fee determination required (KRS Chapter 431)

**Right to Remain Silent:**
- Defendant has no obligation to make statement
- Anything said can be used against defendant
- Right against self-incrimination (Section 11, Kentucky Constitution; Fifth Amendment)

**Right to Know Charges:**
- Judge must inform defendant of charges filed
- Defendant entitled to copy of complaint/warrant
- Nature and cause of accusation must be explained in understandable terms

**Right to Preliminary Hearing (felonies only):**
- If defendant in custody on felony charge not yet indicted, preliminary hearing must be held within 10 days after arraignment
- If defendant released on bond, preliminary hearing must be held within 20 days
- Purpose: determine whether probable cause exists to continue holding defendant or maintaining bond conditions

**Right to Reasonable Bail:**
- RCr 3.05: Judge shall release defendant on personal recognizance or admit to bail if offense is bailable
- Court reviews bail/bond and considers whether it should be changed
- Factors considered: nature of offense, criminal history, flight risk, danger to community, ties to community, employment, residence stability

**Presumption of Innocence / Burden of Proof:**
- Defendant presumed innocent
- Commonwealth must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt (KRS 500.070(1))
- No inference of guilt may be drawn from mere accusation

### Bail Determination

**Standard Pretrial Release Schedule (Supreme Court Order 2025-22)**

Defendants assessed as low risk for failure to appear and low or moderate risk for new criminal activity are eligible for release on recognizance under statewide schedule, with numerous exceptions including:

Ineligible Defendants:
- Charges listed in specified offense schedule (Appendix A - serious violent crimes)
- Alcohol intoxication in public place (released per specific protocol)
- DUI 1st offense (detained minimum 6 hours)
- DUI causing death or serious physical injury (not eligible for schedule release)
- Indictment warrant arrests
- Extradited defendants
- Defendants who decline pretrial services interview
- Contempt of court arrests
- Violation of release conditions
- Offenses during declared emergency/disaster/civil unrest
- Charges with alleged victim (special no-contact condition imposed)

**Judicial Discretion Factors (RCr 4.02; KRS 431.066):**

For defendants not eligible under pretrial release schedule or when judicial review required:

Primary Considerations:
- Nature and circumstances of offense charged
- Weight of evidence against defendant
- Defendant's criminal record
- Defendant's record of appearance at court proceedings or flight to avoid prosecution
- Ties to community (employment, family, residence, length of residence)
- Defendant's character, reputation, mental condition
- Whether defendant poses danger to specific person or community
- Financial resources for posting bond

Special Provisions for Domestic Violence/Sexual Assault Cases (KRS 431.064):
- Court must review facts of arrest before releasing defendant charged with KRS 508 (assault), KRS 510 (sex crimes), or protective order violations
- Court must impose conditions protecting alleged victim
- Conditions may include: no-contact orders, stay-away provisions, GPS monitoring, surrender of firearms, substance abuse testing

Bond Types Available:
- Release on own recognizance (ROR)
- Unsecured bond (defendant signs promise to pay if fails to appear, but posts no money upfront)
- Cash bond (full amount posted)
- Property bond (real property pledged)
- Surety bond (commercial bail bonding outlawed in Kentucky - not available)
- Conditional release (specific conditions imposed: GPS, home incarceration, substance abuse treatment, mental health treatment, regular reporting)

**24-Hour Bond Review:**

If defendant not released under pretrial schedule and judge not immediately available, pretrial services must provide circuit judge, district judge, or trial commissioner with risk assessment and release recommendations within 24 hours of booking. Judge reviews and sets appropriate bond.

## PHASE 4: DISTRICT COURT PROCEEDINGS (Misdemeanors and Preliminary Hearings)

### Jurisdiction and Case Assignment

**District Court Jurisdiction:**
- All misdemeanors (offenses punishable by fine only or jail time up to 12 months)
- Preliminary hearings in felony cases
- Traffic offenses, violations, infractions

**Daviess District Court Structure:**
- Part of 6th Judicial District
- Handles initial appearances, arraignments, pretrial conferences, trials, and sentencing for misdemeanor cases
- Handles only preliminary hearings for felonies before transfer to circuit court

Misdemeanor cases prosecuted by County Attorney (elected, four-year term).

### Arraignment - Misdemeanor Cases

**Timing:**
- Held at first court appearance after arrest or citation issuance
- Defendant may waive personal appearance if represented by attorney (judge's discretion)
- Video arraignment permissible

**Arraignment Procedure:**
- Formal reading of charges
- Advisement of rights (as detailed in Phase 3)
- Entry of plea: guilty, not guilty, or guilty but mentally ill
- Setting of bond if not previously determined
- Appointment of counsel if defendant indigent
- Setting of pretrial conference date or trial date

**Plea Entry:**
- Most defendants enter "not guilty" plea to preserve all rights
- Guilty plea at arraignment possible if defendant waives rights knowingly, intelligently, and voluntarily (Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238 (1969))
- Guilty plea must not be product of coercion or undue influence
- Court must establish factual basis for guilty plea
- Defendant must understand consequences: loss of trial rights, appeal rights, potential collateral consequences (scholarship loss, housing, immigration, enhanced penalties for future offenses)

### Pretrial Conference - Misdemeanor Cases

**Purpose and Procedure:**
- Opportunity for defense and County Attorney to negotiate resolution
- Defense reviews discovery (police reports, witness statements, physical evidence, videos)
- County Attorney may make plea offer
- Case may be continued for additional discovery, investigation, or negotiation
- Defendant's presence may be waived (attorney appears on behalf) if judge permits

**Potential Outcomes:**
- Plea agreement reached and case resolved
- Case continued for further negotiation
- Case set for trial
- Dismissal if prosecution cannot proceed
- Diversion program entry (if eligible)

**Pretrial Diversion (Misdemeanors):**
- Available for eligible defendants (typically first-time offenders on non-violent charges)
- Application made after charging but before trial
- Requires Commonwealth recommendation
- Conditions imposed: community service, restitution, classes, substance abuse treatment, mental health treatment, regular reporting
- Successful completion results in dismissal
- Waiver of speedy trial required
- Violation of conditions results in prosecution resuming

### Trial - Misdemeanor Cases in District Court

**Trial Types:**
- Bench trial (judge decides guilt/innocence)
- Jury trial (six-person jury in district court for misdemeanors)

**Speedy Trial Rights:**
- Trial of persons in custody shall be held as promptly as reasonably possible
- Constitutional right to speedy trial (Section 11, Kentucky Constitution; Sixth Amendment)
- Unreasonable delay can result in dismissal

**Trial Procedure (Brief Overview):**
- Jury selection (if jury trial)
- Opening statements (Commonwealth first, then defense)
- Commonwealth's case-in-chief (witnesses, exhibits)
- Defense motion for directed verdict (if Commonwealth fails to establish prima facie case)
- Defense case (defendant has no obligation to present evidence or testify)
- Rebuttal by Commonwealth
- Closing arguments
- Jury instructions (or legal analysis if bench trial)
- Verdict
- Judgment entered on verdict

**Burden of Proof:**
- Commonwealth must prove every element beyond reasonable doubt (KRS 500.070(1))
- Defendant has no burden to prove innocence
- Reasonable doubt standard is highest burden in American law

**Sentencing - Misdemeanors:**
- Typically imposed immediately upon conviction
- Judge has discretion within statutory limits
- Misdemeanors: fine and/or up to 12 months jail
- Class A Misdemeanor: up to 12 months, fine up to $500
- Class B Misdemeanor: up to 90 days, fine up to $250
- Probation available as alternative to incarceration
- Conditional discharge available

### Preliminary Hearing - Felony Cases in District Court

**Purpose:**
Determine whether probable cause exists to believe:
1. A felony was committed, and
2. Defendant committed it

This is NOT a determination of guilt, only whether sufficient evidence exists to justify continued detention or bond and to proceed to circuit court.

**Timing Requirements:**
- Defendant in custody: within 10 days after arraignment (RCr 3.14)
- Defendant released on bond: within 20 days after arraignment
- Continuances permitted for good cause

**Hearing Procedure:**
- Commonwealth presents witnesses and evidence
- Defense may cross-examine witnesses
- Defense may present evidence but typically does not (preserves trial strategy, avoids revealing defense)
- Hearsay admissible if within recognized exception
- Lower evidentiary standard than trial

**Outcomes:**
- Probable cause found: defendant bound over to grand jury; case proceeds to circuit court
- Probable cause not found: defendant released; Commonwealth may still present case to grand jury
- If probable cause found and defendant in custody, bond reviewed and may be modified

**Waiver:**
Defendant may waive preliminary hearing. Common reasons: already indicted by grand jury, negotiating plea agreement, avoiding revealing Commonwealth's evidence.

**Jurisdiction Transfer:**
After preliminary hearing or waiver, case file transferred to circuit court for grand jury presentation and circuit court proceedings.

## PHASE 5: GRAND JURY (Felonies Only)

### Daviess County Grand Jury Structure

**C6 Local Rule 2.06 - Grand Jury Schedule:**
Grand jury impaneled on first Tuesday of January and first Tuesday of July (or next day thereafter if legal holiday) in months without jury trial periods. Each grand jury meets thereafter on first Tuesday of each month for six months. Sessions typically 2-4 days, not exceeding statutory limit of 20 total days per jury's service.

### Composition and Selection

**Grand Jury Membership:**
- 12 citizens selected from jury pool
- Must meet standard jury qualifications (adult, citizen, county resident, mentally competent, no disqualifying felony convictions)
- Selected through random process from master jury list
- Serve six-month term

**Foreperson:**
- Selected by grand jurors or appointed by court
- Administers oaths to witnesses
- Signs indictments
- Reports to court

### Grand Jury Function and Authority

**Role (RCr 5.01):**
- Inquire into crimes committed within county
- Determine whether probable cause exists to charge defendant with felony
- Return indictments when evidence supports felony charges
- Issue reports on public matters within jurisdiction
- Investigate public officials and public institutions

**Independence:**
- Grand jury functions independently of prosecution and court
- Grand jury determines which witnesses to hear
- Grand jury may subpoena witnesses and documents
- Grand jury may exclude Commonwealth Attorney while questioning witnesses (RCr 5.02)

### Grand Jury Proceedings

**Secrecy (RCr 5.18):**

Persons Permitted in Grand Jury Room:
- Grand jurors
- Attorney(s) for Commonwealth
- Witness under examination
- Stenographer or recording device operator appointed by Commonwealth Attorney
- Interpreter (if needed)
- Parent, guardian, or custodian of minor witness or witness under disability

Persons Prohibited:
- Defense attorney
- Defendant (unless testifying as witness)
- Judge
- Public
- Any other person

Deliberation and Voting:
- ONLY grand jurors present during deliberations and voting
- No one else permitted

Violation of secrecy rules may result in contempt citation.

**Procedure:**

Commonwealth Attorney's Role (KRS 15.725; RCr 5.14):
- Presents cases to grand jury
- May attend witness testimony at grand jury's discretion
- Grand jury may exclude Commonwealth Attorney during witness questioning
- Commonwealth Attorney drafts indictments for grand jury consideration
- No obligation to present case if Commonwealth Attorney believes insufficient evidence

Witness Testimony:
- Witnesses testify under oath
- No cross-examination
- Grand jurors may question witnesses
- No right to have attorney present during testimony (attorney may wait outside)
- Witness may step out to consult attorney
- Target of investigation may request to testify but grand jury not required to permit

Evidence Standard:
- Hearsay admissible
- Lower standard than trial (probable cause, not beyond reasonable doubt)
- Grand jury determines whether evidence sufficient to charge

Voting:
- 9 of 12 grand jurors must concur to return indictment
- Vote by secret ballot

### Indictment

**Content Requirements (RCr 6.10):**
- Written accusation by grand jury
- Charges defendant with felony
- Must state facts and elements of offense with sufficient specificity to inform defendant of charges and permit preparation of defense
- Must be signed by foreperson
- Endorsed "A True Bill"

**Types of Charges:**
- Single count (one offense)
- Multiple counts (several offenses charged separately)
- Multiple defendants (several persons charged in same indictment if offenses related)

**Filing:**
- Indictment filed with circuit court clerk
- Defendant served with copy
- Arrest warrant issued if defendant not already in custody or released on bond
- If defendant on bond from district court, bond continues unless circuit court modifies

**No True Bill:**
If grand jury finds insufficient evidence, returns "No True Bill" and matter dismissed. Commonwealth may re-present to different grand jury if new evidence develops.

**Statute of Limitations (KRS 500.050):**
- Felonies: No limitation period (may be prosecuted anytime)
- Misdemeanors: Must be commenced within one year after commission
- Special provisions for certain sex offenses involving minors

## PHASE 6: CIRCUIT COURT PROCEEDINGS (Felonies and Misdemeanor Appeals)

### Jurisdiction and Organization

**Circuit Court Jurisdiction:**
- All felony prosecutions (crimes punishable by more than 12 months imprisonment)
- Appeals from district court misdemeanor convictions
- Original jurisdiction over certain specialized matters

**Daviess Circuit Court - 6th Judicial Circuit:**
Operates in two divisions (Division I and Division II) per C6 Local Rule 1.04:
- Each division has jurisdiction over both civil and criminal cases
- Master Commissioner and Deputy Master Commissioner assigned to each division
- Civil and criminal cases divided equally between divisions "as possible"
- Each judge may preside over, hear, and determine any case in other division when judges agree or when one judge sick, absent, or otherwise unavailable
- Either judge may vary, discontinue, or extend any trial period whenever business requires
- Either judge has power to assign for trial all cases in division for such days as judge's discretion deems proper for convenient dispatch of division business

### Arraignment - Felony Cases in Circuit Court

**Timing (C6 Local Rule 2.01):**
Arraignment and initial pretrial conferences held commencing 8:30 AM on first Thursday and Friday next after beginning of trial period.

**Procedure:**
- Formal reading of charges from indictment
- Defendant must be personally present (cannot waive appearance)
- Rights advisement (as in district court, repeated for emphasis and record)
- Entry of plea: not guilty, guilty, guilty but mentally ill, or Alford plea (guilty plea while maintaining innocence)

**District Court Rule:**
District court lacks jurisdiction to accept guilty plea in felony case at district court arraignment. District court always enters "not guilty" plea and transfers case to circuit court.

**Circuit Court Plea Entry:**
- Most defendants enter "not guilty" plea at circuit court arraignment
- Case set for pretrial conference
- Discovery initiated
- Defendant may plead guilty at arraignment if knowingly, intelligently, and voluntarily waiving rights
- Court must establish factual basis
- Guilty plea at arraignment typically occurs only with pre-negotiated plea agreement

### Pre-Trial Procedures and Motion Practice

**C6 Local Rule 2.02 - Criminal Motion Docket:**

Criminal motions heard each morning at 8:30 AM on Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday.

Hearings estimated to last more than 30 minutes set by calling judge's office. Generally set at 1:30 on first available afternoon.

**Common Pretrial Motions:**

**Discovery Motions:**
- RCr 7.24 governs criminal discovery
- Defense entitled to: police reports, witness statements, defendant's statements, scientific reports, expert witness information, physical evidence, exculpatory evidence (Brady material), impeachment evidence (Giglio material)
- Mutual disclosure: defense must provide reciprocal discovery of alibi witnesses, insanity defense notice, expert witnesses

**Motion to Suppress Evidence (RCr 8.32):**
- Challenges legality of search, seizure, or arrest
- Challenges admissibility of defendant's statements
- Hearing held on motion (evidentiary hearing with witness testimony)
- Burden on defendant to establish standing
- Burden on Commonwealth to establish lawfulness of search/seizure
- Exclusionary rule: illegally obtained evidence suppressed

**Motion to Dismiss:**
- Insufficient indictment
- Lack of jurisdiction
- Statute of limitations expired
- Double jeopardy
- Speedy trial violation
- Prosecutorial misconduct

**Motion for Bill of Particulars (RCr 6.22):**
- Requests Commonwealth provide more specific information about charges
- Must be filed before arraignment or afterward with court permission
- May be amended as justice requires

**Motion for Change of Venue (RCr 8.30):**
- Requests trial be moved to different county
- Grounds: prejudice in community prevents fair trial, convenience of parties and witnesses
- Requires substantial showing of prejudice

**Motion to Sever (RCr 9.16):**
- Requests separate trials when multiple counts or multiple defendants joined in single indictment
- Must be made before jury sworn (bench trial: before evidence received)
- Court orders severance if prejudice would result from joinder

**Competency Evaluation (KRS 504.080):**
- Court may order mental examination (up to 30 days)
- Determines whether defendant competent to stand trial (understands proceedings, can assist counsel)
- If incompetent but substantial probability of attaining competency, court may commit for treatment (60 days or until competent)

**Motion in Limine:**
- Requests court exclude specific evidence from trial
- Heard immediately before trial

**C6 Local Rule 2.02(4)(d) - Motion Requirements:**

Written motions served at least five (5) days before hearing unless different time fixed by CR, applicable statute, or court order.

All motions going to merits or requiring citation of authority must be accompanied by brief but complete statement of grounds for motion and memorandum of points and authorities. Failure to comply may be grounds for overruling motion.

Party properly served with motion or application must file response containing statement of grounds for opposing motion with memorandum of points and authorities. Responses filed at or prior to time specified in notice of hearing. Failure to file response may be grounds for sustaining motion, but time for filing response may be extended for good cause shown.

### Pretrial Conferences (RCr 8.26; C6 Local Rule 2.03)

**Purpose:**
- Narrow issues for trial
- Consider guilty pleas or plea negotiations
- Address discovery disputes
- Set motion deadlines
- Determine witness and exhibit lists
- Establish trial scheduling
- Address any matters tending to expedite disposition

**Procedure:**
- Informal meeting between defense attorney and Commonwealth Attorney
- May occur in chambers with judge or outside court
- Defendant not required to attend unless entering plea
- Multiple pretrial conferences common in complex cases

**Scheduling Order (C6 Local Rule 2.03):**
Upon motion of either party or on court's own initiative, scheduling order may be provided establishing deadlines for:
- Discovery completion
- Disclosure of experts
- Pretrial motions
- Pre-trial conference attendance
- Matters subsequent to getting case ready for trial docket

Pretrial conference pursuant to CR 16 may include consideration of scheduling order.

### Assignment of Jury Trials

**C6 Local Rule 2.04 - Assignment of Jury Trials:**

Motions to set jury trials held approximately two months before month with available trial dates. Clerk of each division sets motions to be heard in that division. Attorney wishing to set case for jury trial may move clerk for listing on motion docket.

Attorneys discouraged from moving to assign for trial before pleadings closed and discovery completed. Motion to assign trial dates should be filed until moving party has completed discovery.

Once case on trial docket with dates certain, court enters trial order outlining exchange of witnesses and exhibits expected to be offered into evidence and other pertinent matters. If after time that time for exchange, additional witnesses or exhibits will be offered, movant must immediately notify opposing counsel. Any objections shall be brought to court's attention at pretrial conference, at which time all motions in limine shall be heard.

Attorneys of record in civil cases shall be present at assignment hearing. If they will designate someone attorney to be present in their stead, they will designate someone attorney in writing.

All attorneys in criminal cases shall be present or have someone in their stead duly authorized.

Attorneys may request case be at time convenient with judge after consultation with opposing counsel.

Court trials may be had at time convenient with judge, attorneys, and parties. May be set by motion at any monthly assignment day or at any time after consultation with judge and opposing counsel.

### Jury Trial Schedules

**C6 Local Rule 2.07 - Jury Trial Periods:**

Jury trial periods held in each division commencing on first Monday of every other month except December.

In even-numbered years, jury trial periods as follows:

**Division I:** January, March, May, July, September, November
**Division II:** February, April, June, August, October

In odd-numbered years, months reversed for each division and alternated annually thereafter on annual basis. Although no jury trials in December, grand jury meets at its designated time that month.

Alternatively, first two weeks devoted to trial of civil cases, and criminal cases set thereafter. May be modified by judge as circumstances require.

### Jury Selection (Voir Dire)

**Jury Composition:**
- 12 jurors in felony cases
- Alternates selected (1-4 depending on case complexity and expected length)
- Selected from jury pool drawn from voter registration and driver's license lists

**Voir Dire Process:**
- Judge and/or attorneys question prospective jurors
- Purpose: determine juror qualifications, bias, ability to be fair and impartial
- Challenges for cause (unlimited): juror has bias, relationship to party, formed opinion, cannot follow law
- Peremptory challenges (limited number): no reason required, but cannot be based on race or gender (Batson v. Kentucky)
- Felony trials: 8 peremptory challenges per side

### Trial - Felony Cases

**Trial Process (Comprehensive):**

**1. Opening Statements:**
- Commonwealth presents opening first
- Outlines evidence Commonwealth intends to present
- Defense may present opening immediately after or defer until after Commonwealth's case
- Defense may waive opening

**2. Commonwealth's Case-in-Chief:**
- Commonwealth presents witnesses and exhibits
- Direct examination by Commonwealth Attorney
- Cross-examination by defense attorney
- Redirect examination
- Re-cross examination
- Court may question witnesses
- Physical evidence, documents, photos introduced as exhibits

**3. Motion for Directed Verdict (RCr 9.24):**
- After Commonwealth rests, defense may move for directed verdict
- Argues Commonwealth failed to establish prima facie case (failed to present evidence on every element)
- Viewed in light most favorable to Commonwealth
- Rarely granted

**4. Defense Case:**
- Defense has no obligation to present case
- Defendant has absolute right not to testify (Fifth Amendment; Section 11, Kentucky Constitution)
- No adverse inference from defendant's silence
- If defense presents case: same procedure as Commonwealth's case (direct, cross, redirect, re-cross)

**5. Commonwealth Rebuttal:**
- Commonwealth may present rebuttal evidence responding to defense case
- Limited to rebuttal of matters raised by defense

**6. Closing Arguments:**
- Commonwealth argues first
- Defense argues
- Commonwealth has rebuttal argument (burden of proof gives final word)
- Arguments are not evidence
- Attorneys may argue reasonable inferences from evidence

**7. Jury Instructions:**
- Parties submit proposed instructions (RCr 9.54)
- Judge determines which instructions given
- Instructions conference held outside jury's presence
- Parties must object to erroneous instructions or waive issue on appeal
- Instructions include: elements of offense, lesser included offenses (if applicable), burden of proof, reasonable doubt definition, presumption of innocence, credibility determinations, unanimity requirement

**8. Jury Deliberations:**
- Jury retires to deliberation room
- Only jurors present during deliberations
- Deliberations secret
- Jurors may request: review of exhibits, read-back of testimony, clarification of instructions
- Verdict must be unanimous (all 12 jurors agree)

**9. Verdict:**
- Guilty or not guilty on each count
- If multiple counts or lesser included offenses, jury completes verdict form
- Polling of jury permitted (each juror asked to confirm verdict)
- If jurors cannot reach unanimous verdict: hung jury, mistrial declared, case may be retried

**10. Post-Verdict Motions:**
- Motion for judgment notwithstanding verdict (RCr 10.24): must be filed within 5 days after verdict return
- Motion for new trial (RCr 10.06): must be served not later than 5 days after verdict return

**Courtroom Decorum (C6 Local Rule 1.07):**
- Attorneys and litigants observe customary and traditional courtesies and decorum
- Refrain from caustic remarks about and personal reference to opposing counsel
- Examination of jurors and witnesses conducted from counsel table or from some other suitable distance except when handling documentary or physical evidence or when hearing impairment or other physical disability requires attorneys take different position
- No one brings food or drink into courtroom
- All attorneys wear proper courtroom attire in all proceedings

## PHASE 7: SENTENCING (Post-Conviction)

### Presentence Investigation

**KRS 532.050 - Mandatory for Felonies:**

No court shall impose sentence on conviction for felony (except capital offense) without first ordering presentence investigation and giving consideration to written report. Defendant may waive completion of report until after sentencing if defendant in custody and ineligible for probation or parole.

**Presentence Report Contents:**
- Defendant's criminal history (local, state, federal)
- Mental health conditions and history
- Substance abuse history
- Family background and relationships
- Educational history
- Employment history
- Military service
- Financial status
- Psychological evaluation (may be ordered)
- Victim impact statement
- Defendant's statement
- Probation officer's sentencing recommendation

**Psychiatric Examination (KRS 532.050):**
Court may order presentence psychiatric examination for period not exceeding 60 days.

**Report Review:**
- Defendant has right to review and contest report (RCr 8.28(1)(4))
- Hearing held if defendant contests factual assertions
- Report aids judge in determining appropriate sentence within statutory limits

### Sentencing Hearing

**Timing:**
Sentence imposed without unreasonable delay. Typically 30-60 days after conviction to allow presentence investigation completion.

**Procedure:**
- Presentence report presented to court
- Commonwealth Attorney argues for appropriate sentence (may include victim statements)
- Defense attorney argues for appropriate sentence
- Defendant may make statement (allocution)
- Court may hear from victims (victim impact statements)
- Judge considers: nature and circumstances of offense, defendant's character and background, need for deterrence, protection of public, rehabilitation of defendant, presentence report recommendations

### Sentencing Options and Limitations

**Felony Classification and Penalties (KRS 532.020; KRS 532.060):**

**Capital Offense:**
- Death penalty or life imprisonment without possibility of parole
- Reserved for murder with aggravating circumstances

**Class A Felony:**
- 20-50 years imprisonment or life imprisonment
- Fine: $1,000-$10,000 (KRS 534.030)

**Class B Felony:**
- 10-20 years imprisonment
- Fine: $1,000-$10,000

**Class C Felony:**
- 5-10 years imprisonment
- Fine: $1,000-$10,000

**Class D Felony:**
- 1-5 years imprisonment
- Fine: $1,000-$10,000

**Persistent Felony Offender (PFO) Enhancement (KRS 532.080):**

Significantly increases sentences for defendants with prior felony convictions:

**PFO 1st Degree:**
- Prior conviction for one or more felonies
- Enhancement: minimum sentence increased to 10 years, maximum to 20 years (for any felony)

**PFO 2nd Degree:**
- Prior conviction for one or more felonies
- Enhancement varies based on underlying conviction

**Violent Offender Status (KRS 439.3401):**
Defendants convicted of specified violent offenses must serve 85% of sentence before parole eligible (vs. 20% for non-violent offenders).

**Probation and Conditional Discharge (KRS 533.020):**

**Probation:**
- Alternative to incarceration
- Available for most felonies (some violent offenses excluded)
- Conditions: regular reporting, employment/education, restitution, community service, substance abuse treatment, mental health treatment, curfew, no contact with victims, warrantless searches, drug testing
- Supervised by probation officer
- Violation of conditions results in revocation hearing and imposition of original sentence

**Conditional Discharge:**
- Court withholds entering judgment of conviction
- Defendant placed on probationary status
- Successful completion: charges dismissed, no conviction on record
- Violation: conviction entered, sentence imposed

**Shock Probation (KRS 439.265):**
- Motion filed not earlier than 30 days nor later than 180 days after defendant incarcerated following conviction
- Court must rule within 60 days of motion filing
- Permits court to reconsider sentence and place defendant on probation
- Typically granted to first-time offenders showing rehabilitation potential

**Restitution (KRS 532.032; KRS 533.020):**
- Court may order defendant pay restitution to victims
- Compensates victims for financial losses resulting from crime
- May be condition of probation or part of sentence
- Includes: medical expenses, lost wages, property damage, funeral expenses, counseling costs

**Concurrent vs. Consecutive Sentences:**
- Concurrent: sentences run simultaneously (defendant serves longest sentence)
- Consecutive: sentences run one after another (defendant serves combined total)
- Court has discretion to order concurrent or consecutive sentences

**Credit for Time Served (KRS 532.120):**
Defendant receives credit for time spent in custody before sentencing (pre-trial detention).

### Death Penalty Cases

**Capital Sentencing (KRS 532.025):**

Bifurcated trial: guilt phase and penalty phase.

**Penalty Phase:**
- Separate proceeding after guilty verdict for capital offense
- Commonwealth presents aggravating circumstances
- Defense presents mitigating circumstances
- Jury determines whether death penalty appropriate

**Aggravating Circumstances (KRS 532.025(2)):**
Must find at least one:
- Murder of peace officer or firefighter
- Murder for hire
- Multiple murders
- Murder during kidnapping, rape, sodomy, burglary, robbery, arson
- Murder of witness to prevent testimony
- Especially heinous, atrocious, or cruel manner
- Other statutory aggravators

**Mitigating Circumstances (KRS 532.025(3)):**
Jury considers any mitigating factors:
- No significant prior criminal history
- Defendant acted under extreme emotional disturbance
- Victim was participant or consented
- Defendant acted under duress or domination
- Diminished capacity
- Youth of defendant
- Any other mitigating factor

**Jury Determination:**
- Unanimity required for death penalty
- If jury cannot unanimously agree on death, sentence is life imprisonment without parole
- Automatic appeal to Kentucky Supreme Court

**Date of Execution (KRS 431.218):**
Court's mandate of death sentence, when confirmed on appeal, fixes execution day as fifth Friday following date of mandate.

## PHASE 8: POST-CONVICTION REMEDIES

### Direct Appeal

**Notice of Appeal (RAP 3):**

**Timing:**
- Must be filed within 30 days after entry of final judgment (criminal cases)
- Final judgment: judgment entered after guilty plea or jury verdict, sentence imposed
- Failure to file timely notice = loss of appeal rights (jurisdictional)

**Clerk Notification (C6 Local Rule 2.05; RCr 12.06):**
Clerk immediately notifies parties of entry of judgments in felony cases, except notification unnecessary for judgment entered pursuant to guilty plea.

**Appellate Process:**
- Notice of appeal filed with circuit court clerk
- Transcript of proceedings prepared (defendant entitled to free transcript if indigent)
- Appellate brief filed (appellant's brief, appellee's brief, reply brief)
- Oral argument (may be waived)
- Court of Appeals renders decision
- Further appeal to Kentucky Supreme Court (by motion for discretionary review)

**Issues Reviewable:**
- Legal errors (jury instructions, evidentiary rulings, constitutional violations)
- Sufficiency of evidence (viewed in light most favorable to Commonwealth)
- Sentencing errors

**Harmless Error Doctrine:**
Not every error requires reversal. Error must have affected defendant's substantial rights and likely affected outcome.

**Guilty Plea Appeals:**
Defendant who pleads guilty generally waives right to appeal except:
- Validity of plea itself (was it knowing, intelligent, voluntary)
- Jurisdiction of court
- Legality of sentence
- Issues preserved by conditional plea agreement

### RCr 11.42 Motion (Collateral Attack)

**Purpose and Scope:**

RCr 11.42 permits prisoner in custody under sentence or defendant on probation, parole, or conditional discharge who claims right to be released to collaterally attack conviction directly by motion in court that imposed sentence.

**Who May File:**
- Must be in custody, on probation, parole, or conditional discharge on conviction being attacked
- Not available for convictions resulting only in fine

**Time Deadlines (RCr 11.42(10)):**
- Must be filed within three (3) years after judgment becomes final
- Judgment becomes final when: final judgment entered by circuit court clerk, or when appellate process on direct appeal complete
- Exceptions for newly discovered evidence, constitutional violations not previously available

**Grounds for Relief:**
- Ineffective assistance of counsel (Strickland standard: deficient performance + prejudice)
- Constitutional violations (illegal search/seizure, coerced confession, Brady violation)
- Newly discovered evidence (material, could not have been discovered with due diligence)
- Actual innocence
- Jurisdictional defects

**Procedure:**
- Motion filed in circuit court where conviction obtained
- Defendant must use court-approved form or follow format requirements
- Clerk provides copy to Commonwealth Attorney (RCr 11.42(4))
- Commonwealth has 20 days from clerk's mailing of notice to file response
- Court may grant or deny hearing
- Court may grant or deny relief
- Appeal to Court of Appeals if relief denied

**Evidentiary Hearing:**
Court must grant evidentiary hearing if motion raises material issues of fact requiring hearing to resolve, unless motion and files/records conclusively show defendant not entitled to relief.

**Not a Substitute for Direct Appeal:**
RCr 11.42 is not substitute for direct appeal. Issues that could have been raised on direct appeal but were not generally cannot be raised in RCr 11.42 motion (with exceptions for ineffective assistance claims and constitutional violations).

### Federal Habeas Corpus (28 U.S.C. § 2254)

**Purpose:**
Federal remedy for state prisoners claiming federal constitutional violations.

**Exhaustion Requirement:**
Must exhaust all state remedies (direct appeal and RCr 11.42) before filing federal habeas corpus petition.

**Time Limitation:**
One year from date state conviction becomes final (includes time for direct appeal and RCr 11.42).

**Grounds:**
Limited to federal constitutional violations (Fourth Amendment, Fifth Amendment, Sixth Amendment, Fourteenth Amendment).

## PHASE 9: EXECUTION OF JUDGMENT

### Confinement

**KRS 532.100 - Place of Confinement:**

**Felonies:**
- Sentences of more than 12 months: Kentucky Department of Corrections (state prison system)
- Sentences of 12 months or less: county jail (at court's discretion)

**Misdemeanors:**
- County jail

**Classification and Placement:**
DOC classifies inmates by security level (minimum, medium, maximum, supermax) and assigns to appropriate facility based on offense, criminal history, disciplinary record, escape risk, program needs.

**Notice of Judgment Ordering Execution (C6 Local Rule; RCr 11.22):**
Sheriff furnished forthwith with two certified copies of judgment ordering confinement or execution of defendant. Sheriff serves defendant and provides certified copy to person in charge of institution of confinement. Sheriff gives notice of return service within 10 days.

### Parole and Probation

**Parole Board (KRS 439.340):**
- Reviews inmates for parole eligibility
- Considers: nature of offense, criminal history, institutional conduct, rehabilitation programming, parole plan, risk to community
- Grants or denies parole

**Parole Eligibility:**
- Non-violent offenders: eligible after serving 20% of sentence
- Violent offenders: eligible after serving 85% of sentence
- Life sentences: minimum 20 years (25 years if convicted after 1998)
- Sex offenders: special provisions under KRS 439.3401

**Probation Supervision:**
- Probation officers supervise defendants on probation
- Regular reporting, drug testing, employment verification, home visits
- Violations reported to court
- Revocation hearing if violations serious

### Expungement and Record Sealing

**Felony Expungement (KRS 431.073):**

Eligible Felonies (Class D only):
- Five years after completion of sentence (including probation, parole)
- No subsequent felony or misdemeanor convictions
- No pending charges
- Eligible offenses limited (violent offenses, sex offenses, DUI generally ineligible)

**Misdemeanor Expungement (KRS 431.078):**
- Class A or B misdemeanor
- Five years after completion of sentence
- Limited to certain offenses
- No subsequent convictions

**Effect of Expungement:**
- Records sealed from public
- Defendant may deny conviction occurred (with limited exceptions for law enforcement, licensing)
- Conviction still considered for sentencing enhancement in future cases

## SPECIALIZED PROCEEDINGS AND NOTABLE PROVISIONS

### Domestic Violence and Assault Cases

**Special Bond Considerations (KRS 431.064):**

Before releasing person arrested for or charged with KRS 508 (assault), KRS 510 (sex crimes), or protective order violation, court must:
- Review facts of arrest and detention
- Make findings on record concerning determination
- May impose conditions protecting alleged victim: no-contact orders, stay-away provisions, GPS monitoring, surrender of firearms, substance abuse testing

### Mental Health Proceedings

**Competency to Stand Trial (KRS 504.060; KRS 504.100):**
- Defendant incompetent if unable to understand nature and consequences of proceedings or unable to assist counsel in defense
- Court-ordered evaluation
- If incompetent: proceedings suspended, defendant may be committed for treatment
- Periodic review of competency status

**Not Guilty by Reason of Insanity (KRS 504.020):**
- Defense must provide notice of intent to raise insanity defense
- Burden on defendant to prove insanity by preponderance of evidence
- Acquittal by reason of insanity results in commitment to mental health facility
- Periodic review for release

**Guilty But Mentally Ill (KRS 504.120):**
- Defendant found guilty but suffering from mental illness at time of offense
- Sentenced as if guilty verdict, but receives mental health treatment during incarceration
- Not a defense; does not reduce culpability

### Juvenile Delinquency Cases

**Jurisdiction:**
- District court has exclusive jurisdiction over juveniles (persons under 18)
- Serious offenses may be transferred to circuit court (youthful offender proceedings)
- Different procedural rules apply (JCRPP - Juvenile Court Rules of Procedure and Practice)

**Youthful Offenders (KRS 635.020):**
- Juveniles charged with serious felonies may be transferred to circuit court
- If convicted, sentenced as adult but with modified sentencing provisions

## CONCLUSION: SYSTEMIC OBSERVATIONS

### Timeline Variability

Criminal case lifecycle in Daviess County varies significantly based on:
- Offense severity (misdemeanor vs. felony)
- Defendant custody status (in custody vs. bonded)
- Case complexity (number of charges, witnesses, evidence)
- Negotiation dynamics (plea agreement vs. trial)
- Court calendar (trial periods, continuances)

Typical timelines:
- Misdemeanor plea: 2-6 months from arrest to disposition
- Misdemeanor trial: 4-12 months from arrest to trial
- Felony plea: 6-18 months from arrest to disposition
- Felony trial: 12-36 months from arrest to trial
- Complex felonies: 24-48+ months

### Constitutional Protections Throughout

Every phase governed by constitutional protections:
- Fourth Amendment: protection against unreasonable searches and seizures
- Fifth Amendment: right against self-incrimination, double jeopardy, due process
- Sixth Amendment: right to counsel, speedy trial, confrontation, compulsory process, jury trial
- Eighth Amendment: protection against excessive bail, cruel and unusual punishment
- Fourteenth Amendment: due process, equal protection

### Practical Realities

**Resource Constraints:**
- Public defender caseloads
- Jail capacity
- Court calendar congestion
- Witness availability

**Plea Negotiations:**
- Vast majority of cases (95%+) resolve through plea agreements
- Trial remains constitutional right but practical exception
- Negotiation leverage influenced by evidence strength, defendant's record, victim cooperation, community impact

**Local Legal Culture:**
- Relationship between Commonwealth Attorney's office and defense bar
- Judicial philosophy and sentencing tendencies
- Community standards and expectations
- Specialized dockets (drug court, mental health court, veterans court)

### Integration with C6 Local Rules

Daviess Circuit Court local rules establish specific procedural requirements layered atop statewide RCr and KRS provisions:
- Specific motion hearing times
- Grand jury and trial period schedules
- Detailed scheduling and filing requirements
- Courtroom decorum standards

These local rules create predictability and efficiency within Daviess County system while maintaining compliance with constitutional and statutory mandates.

### Final Note on Complexity

This analysis provides comprehensive overview of criminal case lifecycle, but criminal procedure is extraordinarily complex. Every case presents unique facts, legal issues, and strategic considerations. Effective representation requires mastery of constitutional law, criminal procedure, evidence law, substantive criminal law, and local practice, combined with investigation, negotiation, and trial skills. The stakes—liberty, reputation, collateral consequences—demand rigorous adherence to procedural protections and zealous advocacy within ethical bounds.
